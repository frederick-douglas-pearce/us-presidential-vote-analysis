# Research: Faithless Electors, 1789–2020

> **Status: COMPLETE.** Compiled 2026-09-06 as the factual basis for Post 5 of the "Counted, Not
> Assumed" series. It is filed here rather than under `social/` because `social/` is gitignored
> working state and this is a **durable, citable record** — the point is never to redo this work.
>
> **The headline finding, and it reverses the framing the post started from.** The 2016 defections are
> usually described (and were described in this post's first two drafts) as seven people acting for
> seven private reasons. They were not. **In two centuries, exactly two people organized an attempt to
> use the Electoral College to change who became president: Henry D. Irwin in 1960 and the Washington
> electors in 2016.** Both solicited electors in other states; both aimed to deny the leader a
> majority; both failed. **Every other faithless elector on record was protesting, not plotting** — and
> two of them said outright that they would have stopped short of deciding the election.
>
> **1960 was the larger operation and is essentially forgotten**: 218 telegrams, a Byrd/Goldwater
> coalition ticket, sworn Senate testimony, and a claimed arithmetic of 200 Republican plus 60
> Democratic electors. 2016 produced seven defections nationally.
>
> **One finding is repo-native** (§3): 1960's Byrd 15 splits AL 6 / MS 8 / OK 1, and **only the
> Oklahoma vote was faithless.** The other 14 were *lawfully unpledged* slates run under state statute
> — a mechanism that exists nowhere today. Our own warehouse shows fifteen votes that look identical
> and cannot, by itself, tell you that fourteen were routine and one was a conspiracy.

**Date:** 2026-09-06 · **Method:** two parallel research agents (motives; calendar/mechanics), plus
direct reads by the parent of the *Chiafalo* slip opinion, the Supreme Court docket, the House
historian's page, and this project's own public API.

## §1 — The two attempts

### 2016 — **VERIFIED**, from the Supreme Court's own statement of facts

*Chiafalo v. Washington*, 591 U.S. ___ (2020), slip op. at 6
(https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/19-465_i425.pdf):

> "All three pledged to support Hillary Clinton in the Electoral College. But as that vote approached,
> they decided to cast their ballots for someone else. **The three hoped they could encourage other
> electors — particularly those from States Donald Trump had carried — to follow their example. The
> idea was to deprive him of a majority of electoral votes and throw the election into the House of
> Representatives.** So the three Electors voted for Colin Powell for President. But their effort
> failed. Only seven electors across the Nation cast faithless votes — **the most in a century**, but
> well short of the goal. Candidate Trump became President Trump."

- **Colin Powell was the deliberate vehicle**, not seven independent preferences.
- **"The most in a century"** is the Court's own phrase — use it; no news citation needed.
- Petitioners: **Peter B. Chiafalo, Levi Jennet Guerra, Esther Virginia John** (caption, slip op. 1).
  Fined **$1,000 apiece** under then-Wash. Rev. Code §29A.56.340 (2016).
- ⚠ **The fourth Washington elector** (who voted for Faith Spotted Eagle) is **not** a petitioner.
  Nothing in the record here establishes what happened to him. **UNVERIFIED — do not assert.**

**The 2016 arithmetic — VERIFIED (derived).** Trump was pledged **306**; the threshold is **270** of
538. Denying a majority required him to fall to ≤269, i.e. **37 defections** from the pledged slate.
**Two** occurred (both Texas). He finished on **304**.
⚠ **Baseline trap:** from the *cast* 304 the figure is **35 more**. 37-from-pledged and 35-from-cast
describe the same fact. State one and stay in it.

### 1960 — **VERIFIED**, and the larger of the two

**Henry D. Irwin**, Republican elector, Bartlesville, Oklahoma. Pledged to Nixon; voted **Byrd** for
President and **Goldwater** for Vice President.

**The aim, contemporaneously — VERIFIED.** *The Evening Star* (Washington DC), **20 Dec 1960, p. 6**,
the day after the electors voted — https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1960-12-20/ed-1/?sp=6:

> "**One Elector Balks** — In fact, one jumped the traces. Though Mr. Nixon carried Oklahoma, Elector
> Henry D. Irwin of Bartlesville insisted on voting for Senator Byrd for President and Senator
> Goldwater, Republican of Arizona, for Vice President. And Senator Byrd collected 14 more votes from
> electors in Mississippi and Alabama **who ran as unpledged to any candidate**. … **The Southerners
> had tried to persuade other electors to go along with their revolt against the major parties, but
> got no takers except Mr. Irwin. They had hoped to prevent Senator Kennedy's getting a clear majority
> of the electoral vote, which would have thrown the presidential election into the House of
> Representatives.**"

**Scale, from sworn testimony — VERIFIED.** Irwin testified before the Senate Judiciary Subcommittee
on Constitutional Amendments, July 1961. *Evening Star*, **14 Jul 1961, p. 3** (J. A. O'Leary) —
https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1961-07-14/ed-1/?sp=3:

> "**Mr. Irwin described in detail how he sent telegrams to all the Republican electors in the States
> carried by Mr. Nixon after the election, and said their replies indicated to him that 200 of them
> would leave Mr. Nixon and vote for the conservative coalition if assurances could be obtained that
> 60 Democratic electors would do likewise. He said these numbers, plus the independent electors in
> the South, would have put over the coalition ticket.**"

Same report: he had announced **in spring 1960**, on filing as an elector candidate, that he would not
support Nixon if nominated; asked about the party-pledge language on his filing document, "**the
witness admitted that perhaps there was such a statement, but contended that it is not properly in the
Oklahoma law and has never been tested.**"

**Initiated by R. Lea Harris**, a Montgomery, Alabama lawyer — **VERIFIED**. *Evening Star*,
**17 Jul 1961, p. 7** (AP) — https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1961-07-17/ed-1/?sp=7:
"Evidence collected by a Senate Judiciary subcommittee indicates that an attempt to spark a revolt
last year by presidential electors was initiated by R. Lea Harris… **There was no testimony that
Senator Harry F. Byrd… or Senator Goldwater… had any knowledge of the behind-the-scenes maneuvering.**"
Corroborated *Evening Star*, **19 Jul 1961, p. 76** (AP) —
https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1961-07-19/ed-1/?sp=76.

**A contemporary legal assessment — VERIFIED.** Albert J. Rosenthal, "The Constitution, Congress, and
Presidential Elections," **67 Mich. L. Rev. 1 (1968)**, inserted into the record by Sen. John Sherman
Cooper at **115 Cong. Rec. 237 (6 Jan 1969)**
(https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1969-pt1/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1969-pt1-2.pdf):

> "**Shortly after election day, one Lea Harris of Montgomery, Alabama, circularized the newly chosen
> electors, urging them to withhold electoral votes from Kennedy (and Nixon as well)… One Republican
> elector, Henry D. Irwin of Oklahoma, sent out further solicitations of his own. In the end, however,
> he alone switched.** … **Irwin claimed to have had the 'tacit support' of the Republican National
> Committee, but on cross-examination it was established that he had garnered little more than vague
> expressions of sympathy** … it is disquieting to speculate on what a better-organized campaign to
> subvert electors might have achieved, or what Messrs. Harris and Irwin themselves might have
> accomplished **if Kennedy had had only, say, two or three instead of thirty-four electoral votes over
> the 269 necessary for a majority.**"

Rosenthal n.89 cites *Hearings on S.J. Res. 1 Before the Subcomm. on Constitutional Amendments of the
Senate Comm. on the Judiciary*, 87th Cong., 1st Sess. **562–656 (1961)** — the primary hearing record,
not yet retrieved. **The single best unfetched source on this topic.**

**A senator naming it as design — VERIFIED.** Sen. Edmund Muskie, **115 Cong. Rec. 202 (6 Jan 1969)**:

> "**beginning in 1948 we began to have a different line of examples, of a clear intention to use this
> constitutional freedom, so-called, for the purpose of frustrating the will of the electorate.** In
> 1960 the proposition was circularized to Kennedy-and-Nixon-pledged electors that they withhold their
> votes so that they could exact a bargain… **So we face this question now not as an isolated
> instance, but as a deliberate design on the part of people to frustrate the popular will.**"

**Consequence — VERIFIED.** Oklahoma legislated in **1961**: a sworn elector oath plus a **$1,000
fine**, 26 Okla. Stat. Ann. §§519–22 (1962), per James C. Kirby, Jr. (the subcommittee counsel who
cross-examined Irwin), "Limitations on the Power of State Legislatures over Presidential Elections,"
**27 Law & Contemp. Probs. 495 (1962)**, n.106. Irwin was **not** punished. State GOP chairman **Henry
Bellmon** called the act "irresponsible" (**ATTRIBUTED**, *Tulsa World*, 7 Jun 1992 — see §10 n.9).

**Irwin's telegram text — ATTRIBUTED, not VERIFIED.** The widely-quoted wording ("I am Oklahoma
Republican elector… Sufficient conservative Democratic electors available to deny labor Socialist
nominee. Would you consider Byrd President, Goldwater Vice President…") rests on an **unfootnoted**
FairVote/Center for Voting and Democracy archive page,
http://archive.fairvote.org/e_college/faithless.htm. Every *substantive* element is independently
corroborated above; the **exact wording is not**. Quote only with the source named.

## §2 — Unpledged slates are NOT faithless electors

This distinction is the most commonly botched fact in the area, including by one CRS report.

**The mechanism was lawful and deliberate — VERIFIED.** Kirby (1962) n.104: **four states** —
Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, Mississippi — "authorize what might be called 'unpledged electors' in the
true sense by allowing electors to be listed under the party label without showing the names of any
candidates for President and Vice President." Mississippi's statute expressly "permits party to enter
slate of unpledged electors in addition to pledged slate" (Miss. Code Ann. §3107.5). Upheld in
*Gray v. Mississippi*, **233 F. Supp. 139, 141–42 (N.D. Miss. 1964)**.

**Congress drew the same line on the floor — VERIFIED.** 115 Cong. Rec. 214 (6 Jan 1969):

> **Mr. CHURCH:** "If Dr. Bailey had been unpledged, then I take it that there would be no basis for
> objecting to the vote that Dr. Bailey cast."
> **Mr. MUSKIE:** "The Senator is correct."

⚠ **A `.gov` conflict — flagged.** **CRS RL32611 n.43** lumps all fifteen together: "In 1960, six
Democratic electors in Alabama and one in Oklahoma voted for candidates other than those to whom they
were pledged, while eight 'unpledged Democratic' electors in Mississippi cast their votes for persons
other than their party's nominees." This **conflicts** with the House historian's count of **one**
faithless elector in 1960 and with the contemporaneous reporting that the Alabama six *ran* unpledged.
**Prefer the House historian and the *Evening Star*.** CRS RL30804's own faithless-elector list names
only Irwin for 1960.

## §4 — The eight pre-2016 instances

Anchor list: House historian, "Electoral College Fast Facts" —
https://history.house.gov/Institution/Electoral-College/Electoral-College/ — **VERIFIED**:

> "There has been one faithless elector in each of the following elections: **1948, 1956, 1960, 1968,
> 1972, 1976, and 1988. A blank ballot was cast in 2000.** In 2016, seven electors broke with their
> state on the presidential ballot and six did so on the vice presidential ballot."

Note the **six vice-presidential** defections in 2016 — rarely mentioned anywhere.

### 1948 — Preston Parks (Tennessee) → Thurmond/Wright
**The weakest "faithless" case in the set.** He appeared on **both** the Democratic and States' Rights
slates. **VERIFIED**, *Evening Star* 13 Dec 1948 p.13
(https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1948-12-13/ed-1/?sp=13): "**two electors on the Truman ballot
were also on the Thurmond ballot**." And 14 Dec 1948 p.6
(https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1948-12-14/ed-1/?sp=6): "Parks… cast his vote for Gov.
Thurmond **as he had said in advance he would.**"
- **Stated reason in his own words: the record doesn't say. UNVERIFIED.** Only his reported advance
  position survives — he "would not vote for President Truman under any circumstances" (*Evening
  Star*, 6 Nov 1948 p.2, AP).
- Tennessee pledge law in 1948: **UNVERIFIED.** Contemporaneous reporting calls it a "tacit pledge."
- Consequence: none.

### 1956 — W. F. Turner (Alabama) → Walter B. Jones / Herman Talmadge
**VERIFIED**, *Evening Star* 18 Dec 1956 p.5 (AP) —
https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1956-12-18/ed-1/?sp=5:

> "fellow electors told Mr. Turner he was under an obligation to vote for Mr. Stevenson, since the
> electors had signed a party loyalty oath. **Mr. Turner replied: 'I have fulfilled my obligations to
> the people of Alabama. I'm talking about the white people.'**"

🚨 **NEVER TRUNCATE THIS QUOTE.** Publishing the first sentence alone materially misrepresents what he
said. The motive was segregationist and the second sentence is what establishes it. Same-page AP
caption notes Jones was the Montgomery circuit judge "**who has banned NAACP activity in the State**."
- The pledge was a **party oath, not a statute** — and *Ray v. Blair*, 343 U.S. 214 (1952) arose from
  **this same Alabama pledge** and expressly reserved whether it was enforceable.
- Consequence: none found.

### 1960 — Henry D. Irwin (Oklahoma) — see §1.

### 1968 — Dr. Lloyd W. Bailey (North Carolina) → Wallace/LeMay
**His own written statement — VERIFIED**, printed at **115 Cong. Rec. 205 (6 Jan 1969)**, dated Rocky
Mount NC, **16 Dec 1968**, entered by Sen. Sam Ervin:

> "**I realize that it is perhaps unusual for an Elector not to vote for his party's nominee, but as an
> Elector it is my duty to place loyalty to my country before loyalty to my political party.**"
> "**The Electoral College is one part of the system of checks and balances which guarantees that the
> minority voice can be heard.**"
> "**in view of the fact that Mr. Wallace polled 46.1% of the vote in my district, I find that I cannot
> support Mr. Nixon until he gives definite evidence that he intends to make the changes which the
> people are demanding.**"

He objects to the Moynihan appointment and to Nixon asking Earl Warren to stay on as Chief Justice
("**This is unthinkable!**"), and cites the Council on Foreign Relations and Dan Smoot's *The Invisible
Government*.
- **North Carolina did not bind its electors in 1968 — VERIFIED.** *Chiafalo* slip op. 17: "the State
  appointing that elector had no law requiring a pledge." NC amended §163-212 in **1969**.
- ⚠ **Bailey's reported later Senate Judiciary testimony: UNVERIFIED — do not assert.**
- Consequence: **the 1969 congressional objection — see §7.**

### 1972 — Roger Lea MacBride (Virginia) → John Hospers / Tonie Nathan
- **Stated reason — ATTRIBUTED only.** Sole contemporaneous source is the Libertarian Party's own
  newsletter, *LP News* No. 12, Jan/Feb 1973, p.1: "an attempt to put principles ahead of party
  politics"; Nixon "has moved the government toward ever greater control over the lives of us all."
  **Name the source in-line or drop the motive.**
- ⚠ **"Tonie Nathan was the first woman to receive an electoral vote": UNVERIFIED.** Asserted by
  *Reason* (Jun 1973) and secondary sources; **no `.gov` source found.** Same class as the Spotted
  Eagle superlative — **do not assert.**
- Virginia binding statute in 1972: **UNVERIFIED** (inferred from a successor section's codifier
  history; Virginia's notarized-oath requirement is **new in 2024** — do not retroject).
- Consequence: none. Vote counted; Agnew read it at the 6 Jan 1973 joint session.

### 1976 — Mike Padden (Washington) → Reagan for President, **Dole as pledged** for VP
*Living public figure — on-the-record statements only.*
- **VERIFIED**, AP via *New York Times*, 15 Dec 1976 p.18: cast "as an exercise of personal conscience,"
  described as "an antiabortion protest." ⚠ Note the criticism quoted is of **Carter**, not Ford.
- **VERIFIED**, *NYT Magazine*, 17 Dec 2000: "**I was using my vote as a protest vote to highlight
  Governor Reagan's support of the pro-life cause, but it was not going to ever be at the expense of
  the election itself… had that happened, I would have voted for Ford.**"
- **VERIFIED**, 2016: "back in 1976… **there wasn't any pledge. There wasn't any restriction at all.**"
- **Washington had no pledge law in 1976 — VERIFIED.** Enacted the following year: *Washington Laws,
  1977 1st Ex. Sess.*, ch. 238 §§1–2. Consequence: none.

### 1988 — Margarette Leach (West Virginia) → reversed her ballots (Bentsen P / Dukakis VP)
- 🚨 **CONTRADICTED: this was NOT a winner-take-all or proportional-allocation protest.** That framing
  was the parent agent's own assumption and is contradicted by her own words. **Do not publish it.**
- **VERIFIED**, *NYT Magazine*, 17 Dec 2000 (same joint interview as Padden):
  > "**When I was an elector, I was most astounded when I found out that I had the privilege of voting
  > some other way, and I thought it was sort of a dangerous situation that the presidency could hang
  > by those kinds of votes, and I wanted to get the message out, and so I cast that vote.**"
  > "**I tried to get the other electors to vote with me**, because I thought a block of electors would
  > make news, but when I saw the horrified looks on the faces of the people I was trying to talk into
  > it, I thought, The party may come after me."
  > "**I don't think I would've had the courage to take the presidency away from anybody.**"
- **West Virginia had no pledge law — VERIFIED.** W. Va. Code §3-1-14 (1963) contains no
  party-nominee requirement and no penalty. Consequence: none.

### 2000 — Barbara Lett-Simmons (District of Columbia) → blank ballot
- **VERIFIED**, NARA: "An elector from the District of Columbia 'cast a blank ballot,' effectively
  abstaining, so DC cast only 2 of its 3 electoral votes."
- **VERIFIED**, *Washington Times* 19 Dec 2000: "**Taxation without representation is tyranny… I have
  cast my ballot for the colonists in the District of Columbia.**" *NYT* 19 Dec 2000: protesting the
  capital's "**colonial status**"; "**civil disobedience of a higher order.**"
- **No congressional objection was raised — VERIFIED** against the primary record: at the 6 Jan 2001
  joint session the DC certificate was read, Gore asked "Is there objection to the certificate just
  counted?" and "**There was no objection.**" Every objection that day concerned Florida.
  *Cong. Rec.*, 6 Jan 2001, p. H34.
- Consequence: none. DC had an oath (D.C. Code §1-1001.08(g)) but no sanction.

## §5 — Categorization

| Year | Elector | Category | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1948 | Preston Parks (TN) | affinity / factional (arguably not faithless) | dual-slate; no stated reason |
| 1956 | W. F. Turner (AL) | protest vs nominee — **segregationist** | own quote |
| **1960** | **Henry D. Irwin (OK)** | **STRATEGIC — change the outcome** | 218 telegrams; sworn testimony |
| 1968 | Lloyd W. Bailey (NC) | protest vs nominee (+ district-mandate + defense of the system) | own written statement |
| 1972 | Roger MacBride (VA) | affinity / ideological | *LP News* only |
| 1976 | Mike Padden (WA) | single-issue protest, **expressly non-decisive** | own words, twice |
| 1988 | Margarette Leach (WV) | **protest against the system itself** | own words |
| 2000 | Lett-Simmons (DC) | protest re DC representation | own words |
| **2016** | **WA three** | **STRATEGIC — change the outcome** | *Chiafalo* |

**Protest is overwhelmingly the norm; strategy is the rare exception.** Six of the eight pre-2016
instances are protest or affinity votes. **Only 1960 and 2016 were attempts to change who became
president, and only those two solicited other electors to move as a bloc.**

**2016 is a scaled-up precedent of 1960 specifically, not of the tradition — and it is the smaller of
the two.** Two structural differences worth stating: (1) 1960 had a **lawful parallel mechanism**
(unpledged slates) that exists nowhere today; 2016 could work only through defection. (2) 1960 produced
no punishment; 2016 produced fines, then *Chiafalo*.

**🔑 Two electors set the limit themselves** — Padden ("not going to ever be at the expense of the
election itself") and Leach ("I don't think I would've had the courage to take the presidency away
from anybody"). This is the strongest available answer to "protest or genuine risk," in the
participants' own words.

## §6 — The statutory calendar

| Cycle | Election Day | → electors vote | → Congress counts | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2000 | Tue 7 Nov | Mon 18 Dec (**41 d**) | Sat 6 Jan 2001 (19 d) | 60 d |
| **2016** | Tue 8 Nov | **Mon 19 Dec (41 d)** | Fri 6 Jan 2017 (18 d) | **59 d** |
| 2020 | Tue 3 Nov | Mon 14 Dec (41 d) | Wed 6 Jan 2021 (23 d) | 64 d |
| 2024 | Tue 5 Nov | Tue 17 Dec (42 d) | Mon 6 Jan 2025 (20 d) | 62 d |

- **Election day** — 3 U.S.C. §1; the date now sits in the §21(1) definition. ECRA **relocated** it and
  **repealed** the old §2 "failure to make choice" escape hatch. Date unchanged.
- **Electors meet** — **3 U.S.C. §7**. ⚠ **ECRA changed "first Monday" → "first Tuesday" after the
  second Wednesday in December** (Pub. L. 117-328 §106(a)). 2016 and 2020 ran under the Monday rule.
- **Congress counts** — 3 U.S.C. §15(a), 6 January at 1 p.m. **Verbatim unchanged by ECRA.** Congress
  has moved it by one-off statute five times (1985, 1989, 1997, 2009, 2013).
- **Old "safe harbor" §5 was superseded, not preserved.** Conclusiveness now attaches to the governor's
  certificate by force of law (§5(c)(1)(A)) rather than by a state opting in. Federal-court
  determinations are "conclusive in Congress" (§5(c)(2)); §5(d) adds an expedited three-judge track.
- **ECRA also raised the objection threshold** from one member of each chamber to **one-fifth of each**
  (§15(d)(2)(B)(i)(II)), and closed the grounds to two: not lawfully certified, or **"not regularly
  given"** (§15(d)(2)(B)(ii)).

**What is legally settled when electors vote:** *who the electors are*. **Not settled:** *how they
vote.* NARA: "**There is no Constitutional provision or Federal law that requires electors to vote
according to the results of the popular vote in their States.**" Between November and December the
outcome is politically certain and legally provisional.

## §7 — Does it inject real uncertainty?

### The Court's own numbers — VERIFIED, *Chiafalo* slip op. 16–17

> "**since the founding, electors have cast some 180 faithless votes** for either President or Vice
> President. But that is **180 out of over 23,000**. And **more than a third of the faithless votes
> come from 1872**, when the Democratic Party's nominee (Horace Greeley) died just after Election Day.
> Putting those aside, faithless votes represent just **one-half of one percent** of the total. …
> **because faithless votes have never come close to affecting an outcome, only one has ever been
> challenged. True enough, that one was counted.**"

Note the Court reaches for **1872** — the same election Post 3 uses, for the same reason.

House historian: faithless electors "**have never determined the outcome of a presidential election.**"

### The margins — VERIFIED totals, **derived** cushions

| Year | Winner EV | Total / Majority | Cushion | Defections needed | Faithless cast |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1876** | Hayes **185** | 369 / **185** | **0** | **1** | none |
| **1796** | Adams 71 | 138 / 70 | **1** | **2** | ≥1 (Samuel Miles, Pa.) |
| **2000** | Bush 271 | 538 / 270 | **1** | **2** | 1 abstention |
| 1916 | Wilson 277 | 531 / 266 | 11 | 12 | none |
| 1968 | Nixon 301 | 538 / 270 | 31 | 32 | 1 (Bailey) |
| **2016** | Trump 304 | 538 / 270 | 34 | **35** (37 from pledged 306) | **7** |

**1876 had a zero cushion** — one defection forces a contingent election, and one defection to Tilden
elects Tilden. None occurred; the year's crisis was fought upstream over rival slates and settled by
the Electoral Commission of 1877.
**1796** is the closest case where faithless voting was actually in play: Pennsylvania Federalist
**Samuel Miles** voted for Jefferson; two such defections would have made Jefferson president.
*Chiafalo* n.7 quotes the contemporary protest (*Gazette of the United States*, 15 Dec 1796):
"**What! do I chuse Samuel Miles to determine for me whether John Adams or Thomas Jefferson is the
fittest man for President… No — I chuse him to act, not to think.**"

### 🔑 §7.1 — The one time it worked: 1836, and it was the vice presidency

**VERIFIED via NARA 1836** (https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/1836):
- Van Buren won the presidency 170 of 294 (majority 148).
- **Virginia's 23 electors voted Van Buren for President but refused Richard M. Johnson for Vice
  President**, casting all 23 for William Smith.
- Johnson finished on **147 — one short of 148**.
- NARA: "Because no candidate for Vice President was elected by the people, **the election was decided
  by the Senate. In that vote, Richard M. Johnson was elected Vice President with 33 votes.** Francis
  Granger received 16 votes."

**The only Senate contingent election in US history.** Faithless electors changed the **procedure**,
not the **result** — which is exactly why "never changed an outcome" survives intact. It is also the
sharpest demonstration that a bloc acting together can move the process.
⚠ A Senate-side citation was not obtained; the *Register of Debates*/Senate Journal for **8 Feb 1837**
is the primary record to chase if one is wanted.

### Contingent election — US Const. amend. XII
President: **House, one vote per state delegation**, **26 of 50** needed. Vice President: **Senate, one
vote per senator.** The Twelfth Amendment's 4 March fallback is superseded by the Twentieth
Amendment §3.

## §9 — *Chiafalo v. Washington* (2020): the legal frame

**VERIFIED** — slip opinion read directly.

- **Held** (syllabus): "**A State may enforce an elector's pledge to support his party's nominee — and
  the state voters' choice — for President.**" Decided **6 July 2020**; **Kagan** for eight, **Thomas**
  concurring in the judgment joined in part by **Gorsuch**.
- "**Nothing in the Constitution expressly prohibits States from taking away presidential electors'
  voting discretion as Washington does**"; the practice "**reflects a longstanding tradition in which
  electors are not free agents; they are to vote for the candidate whom the State's voters have
  chosen.**"
- ***Ray v. Blair***, 343 U.S. 214, 228 (1952) upheld a pledge requirement, rejecting the claim that the
  Constitution "**demands absolute freedom for the elector to vote his own choice**" — but **reserved**
  whether a state may enforce one **by sanction**. *Chiafalo* closes that **68-year** gap.
- **Circuit split resolved:** the Tenth Circuit had held the opposite in *Baca v. Colorado Dept. of
  State*, 935 F.3d 887 (2019) — Colorado could **not** remove its elector. "We granted certiorari to
  resolve the split."
  ⚠ **Docket fact — VERIFIED:** the two cases were consolidated on cert (17 Jan 2020) and then
  **de-consolidated 10 March 2020** ("This case is no longer consolidated with No. 19-518"), argued
  the same day (13 May 2020) and **decided separately**. Do not call them one case.
- **Scale** (slip op. 5): **32 states + DC** have pledge laws; **15** back them with a sanction; states
  began adding sanctions "**about 60 years ago**" (≈1960 — i.e. immediately after Irwin).
  "**Almost all of them immediately remove a faithless elector from his position, substituting an
  alternate whose vote the State reports instead.** A few States impose a monetary fine."
- **🔑 n.3:** "**Since the events in this case, Washington has repealed the fine. It now enforces
  pledges only by removing and replacing faithless electors.**" Wash. Rev. Code §29A.56.090(3) (2019).

**Consequence worth carrying forward:** in most binding states **a faithless vote never reaches
Congress at all** — the elector is replaced and a compliant vote is transmitted. Had the 2016
Washington three acted under today's law, there would be **no Powell votes in the Archives' notes**,
and the 2016 correction this project had to make would not exist.
