### Publishing (`posts/`, `social/`, `tooling/`) — a second, non-pipeline workstream

The repo has a **writing** side that shares nothing with the data pipeline except its subject, and it has its own conventions and CI gates. Findings are published as the **"Counted, Not Assumed"** blog series, live at <https://frederick-douglas-pearce.github.io/blog/>.

- **[`posts/`](posts/) is tracked and holds published posts only**; its [README](posts/README.md) is the authoritative spec for frontmatter, share cards, the Prettier gate, and the publish path. Read it before touching anything under `posts/`.
- **`social/` is git-ignored working state** — drafts, the candidate ledger, the series outline. Deliberate: a half-finished post about a claim that turns out to be wrong should not be in a public repo's history. Two exceptions are tracked: `social/images/` (rendered OG cards plus their committable TOML briefs) and the published post itself, which moves to `posts/`.
- **Verified research for a post lands in [`.claude/specs/`](.claude/specs/), not `social/`** — the one place the writing workstream writes into the pipeline's tree, and deliberately so: `social/` is gitignored, so a costly primary-source pass parked there is not durable. [`.claude/specs/research-faithless-electors.md`](.claude/specs/research-faithless-electors.md) is the first (Post 5), following the house style of `research-pv-source.md` / `research-census-source.md` including **load-bearing evidence labels** (VERIFIED / ATTRIBUTED / UNVERIFIED / CONTRADICTED, never laundered between columns — several widely-repeated claims land in the bottom two rows, one of them contradicted by the subject's own words). Its headline: in two centuries **exactly two people organized an attempt to change a president through the Electoral College** — Irwin in 1960, the Washington three in 2016 — everyone else was protesting. One finding is repo-native: 1960's Byrd 15 splits AL 6 / MS 8 / OK 1 and **only the Oklahoma vote was faithless**, the other 14 being lawfully *unpledged* slates, so the fact table holds fifteen structurally identical rows it cannot itself tell apart. **Read §10 before quoting anything from it** — fourteen source-reliability flags, including two stale `.gov` pages still serving pre-ECRA text, a CRS report that conflicts with the House historian, a hijacked domain cited in a 2020 Supreme Court brief, and a **do-not-truncate warning on a 1956 quote whose second sentence is what establishes the motive**. The distinction from `social/candidates.md` is durability and provenance, not subject: the ledger tracks *claims and their status*, this records *the evidence and what failed to verify*.
- **`tooling/`** holds the four publishing scripts: `publish-to-pages.py` (frontmatter transform + OG resolution + content-compare + the shared-namespace provenance guard of [D056](.claude/specs/decisions.md), syncing to a separate Jekyll Pages repo), `check-og-cards.py` (the PR guard, which reuses the publisher's own `build_plan` rather than re-deriving its rules — and so covers everything *that* enforces, but structurally not the namespace guard, which needs a Pages checkout it deliberately does not have), `check-humanizer-pass.py` (the humanizer guard, below), and `render-og-card.py` (1200×630 cards from a TOML brief).
- **A humanizer pass is required before a post merges** (#258, ported from `claude-code-sessions`). After moving the finished draft into `posts/` on the PR branch, run the [humanizer skill](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) over it, land its edits as their own commit, and record the skill version in the post's `humanizer_pass` frontmatter. `tooling/check-humanizer-pass.py` gates on the field being **recorded**, never on the prose — the skill's "When not to act" rules need judgment a pattern lint cannot supply. Two non-version values are valid and counted separately: `none` (a pass deliberately declined — open-ended, the actionable count) and `predates` (a **closed set** the guard enforces by filename: the four posts published before the convention, so a new post cannot use it). Like `og_card_source`, the field is upstream-only and stripped on publish. [`posts/README.md`](posts/README.md) is the full statement.
- **Four of the CI workflows are publishing gates**, not pipeline CI: `pages-sync.yml` (cross-repo push on merge to `main`), `og-card-guard.yml`, `humanizer-guard.yml`, and `prettier.yml`. The Prettier gate covers **`posts/` only** and pins the *exact* versions the Pages site pins — caret ranges would let it drift a version away from the site's, which is the one way it can pass here and fail there.
- **Two editorial guardrails are absolute.** Nothing critical of a data source is ever published (findings about the National Archives, MIT Election Lab, or UCSB go to them privately via the ledger's `outreach` lane) — and every historical claim is checked before publishing. Both are enforced by review, not by CI.
- **The `marketer` agent drafts public-facing copy; the parent agent handles anything landing in the repo itself** (README, commit messages, PR bodies). Keep that split — it is in the agent's own definition. Agent output is saved verbatim as `social/drafts/<date>-<slug>.raw.md` alongside the edited version, because the raw-vs-shipped diff is what calibrates the agent.
